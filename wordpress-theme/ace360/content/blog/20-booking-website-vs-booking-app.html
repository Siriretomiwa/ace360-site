If you work by appointment, letting clients book online saves phone calls during your work and stops bookings slipping away when you cannot answer. The next question is where they book: on a booking platform, or on your own website? Both have a place.

[[clip:missed-call|Hands busy, phone ringing: a missed booking]]

Why book online at all?

A missed call rarely leaves a message. Online booking catches that client, even when you cannot pick up.

Option 1: a booking platform

Marketplaces and booking apps list many businesses in one place. Clients search the platform, compare and book.

  • Pros: extra visibility to people who browse the platform; quick to start; reviews in one place.
  • Cons: many charge a commission per new client or per booking, or a monthly fee; your listing sits next to competitors; the platform owns much of the client relationship and data; your own brand is less visible.

Option 2: booking on your own website

A booking system built into your site shows your services, prices and real free times. Clients book directly with you.

  • Pros: no commission per booking; clients stay on your site and see only you; you keep the client data (handled under the GDPR); you control reminders, deposits and cancellation rules.
  • Cons: you need to bring people to your site yourself, through Google, your Business Profile, social media and returning clients.

Combining both

Many businesses use a platform for discovery and their own site for returning clients. Put your own booking link everywhere you control: your Google Business Profile, your Instagram bio, your email signature and your reminder messages. Over time, more bookings come in without commission.

What to look for in any booking system

  • Different durations per service, and booking per staff member.
  • Your real working hours, breaks and days off.
  • Confirmation emails with a calendar invite, and reminders.
  • Clients can move or cancel within your rules.
  • Optional deposit with iDEAL to reduce no-shows.
  • Export of your client data if you ever switch.
