If you sell online to customers in the Netherlands, one payment method matters more than any other: iDEAL. Dutch shoppers use it to pay straight from their bank account, confirming the payment in their banking app. A store that only offers cards feels unfamiliar to many of them.

How iDEAL works for the customer

  1. At checkout they choose iDEAL (and, depending on the setup, their bank).
  2. Their banking app opens, or they scan a QR code.
  3. They confirm the payment, and they are sent back to your store.

The money comes from their bank account directly, so there is no card number to type and no credit check.

How you add it to your store

You do not connect to every Dutch bank yourself. You use a payment provider (PSP), such as Mollie, Stripe, Adyen or Buckaroo; on Shopify you can use Shopify Payments. The provider:

  • checks your business when you sign up (company registration, ID, bank account);
  • gives you a plugin or setting for WooCommerce or Shopify;
  • pays out to your business bank account, usually within a few working days.

The technical connection is often quick. The account check can take a few days, so apply well before launch.

Costs

iDEAL is usually charged per successful payment, often a fixed amount and typically lower than card fees. Each provider has its own prices, which change from time to time, so check their current pricing page.

iDEAL becomes Wero

iDEAL is being moved step by step into Wero, the payment system of the European Payments Initiative. Since January 2026 checkouts show a combined “iDEAL | Wero” logo, and the full move is planned to run until the end of 2027. For most stores the payment provider handles the change, so choose one that actively supports Wero and follow their updates.

Checkout mistakes that cost Dutch orders

  • iDEAL hidden below the cards. For a Dutch audience it should be the first or most visible option.
  • Shipping costs shown only at the end. Show them in the cart.
  • Forced account creation. Let people check out as a guest.
  • Prices excluding VAT for consumers. Dutch consumers expect prices including VAT.
  • Never tested on a phone, including the jump to the banking app and back.

Rules for selling to consumers

Consumers in the EU generally have a 14-day right of withdrawal for online purchases, the total price including VAT and shipping must be clear before they pay, and the order button must make it clear they are committing to pay. General information, not legal advice.
