Hiring a web designer in the Netherlands: 7 questions to ask

Looking for an English-speaking web designer in the Netherlands? Seven questions that show you who you are dealing with, from the total price and ownership of your domain to Dutch-specific needs like iDEAL and KvK details.

WhatsApp LinkedIn

In short

  • Get every item and one total in writing
  • See the design before anything is built
  • Domain, hosting and logins in your name

Contents

Finding a web designer is easy. Finding one who builds what you need, at a price you understood up front, and who still answers your email a year later, takes a few good questions. Here are seven to ask, including a few that are specific to doing business in the Netherlands.

1. Can we work in English?

If English is your working language, make sure the whole project can run in it: calls, the quote, the design feedback and the handover guide. Ask also whether they can deliver Dutch pages if you need them for local customers.

2. What exactly is included, and what is the total?

A quote that just says “website” leaves room for surprises. Ask for the number of pages, the functions (contact form, booking, store), who provides text and photos, how many feedback rounds, what is not included, and one total. Ask whether the price includes 21% Dutch VAT; many Dutch quotes do not.

3. Will I see the design before it is built?

Changing a design takes minutes; changing a finished site can take days. Ask to see a design of the key pages first.

4. Whose name are the domain, hosting and logins in?

They should be in yours. If the designer owns your domain, moving away later can be difficult. Ask to get every login at handover.

5. Do you know the Dutch specifics?

  • KvK number and the other required business details on the site;
  • a privacy statement under the GDPR (AVG) and cookie consent;
  • iDEAL (now co-branded with Wero) for online payments;
  • Dutch and English versions set up properly for search engines.

6. What happens after launch?

Who installs updates, how often are backups made, who do you call when something breaks, and what does that cost? Is maintenance optional, and can you cancel monthly?

7. When does it go live, and what if it runs late?

A launch date in the quote keeps the project on track. Ask what happens if it slips: do you pay more if the delay is on their side?

Get it in writing

Good answers count when they are in the quote. That protects both of you.
